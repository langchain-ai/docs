Insights automatically analyzes your traces to detect usage patterns, common behaviors and failure modes—without requiring you to manually review thousands of individual traces. Insights appears as a new tab within any tracing project.

<Note>Insights is in Beta and under active development. To provide feedback or use this feature, reach out to the [LangSmith team](https://forum.langchain.com/c/help/langsmith/8).</Note>

## Prerequisites

An OpenAI API key: Generate this from the [OpenAI dashboard](https://platform.openai.com/account/api-keys).


## Run your first insights job

In the [LangSmith UI](https://smith.langchain.com):

1. Navigate to **Tracing Projects** in the left-hand menu and select your tracing project.
2. Click **+New** to create a new insights job.
3. Enter a name for your job.
4. Store your OpenAI API key as a [workspace](/langsmith/administration-overview#workspaces) secret before creating the job. Click the <Icon icon="key" /> key icon on the top right of the Insights job creation pane.
5. Click **Create**.

Insights jobs take ~15 min to complete.

<Note>Running insights on 1,000 traces typically costs \$0.50-\$1.00 in OpenAI API calls.</Note>

## Understand your results
Once your job has completed, you can navigate to the **Insights** tab to see your traces organized into a hierarchical structure. This example sampled 500 threads from Chat LangChain over the past week.

![Example of the Insights tab with results for chat-langchain-v3. Provides a table with different categories.](/langsmith/images/insights-job-results.png)

The **Categories** column shows how your traces are distributed across the top-level categories that the Insights job discovered.

![Navigating through Insights results by clicking through categories and subcategories to drill down into specific trace patterns](/langsmith/images/insights-nav.gif)

### Top-level categories
Your traces are automatically grouped into top-level categories that represent the broadest patterns in your data. In the Chat LangChain example, traces are grouped by the topic of the user's conversation with the assistant.

The distribution bars show how frequently each pattern occurs, which makes it easy to spot behaviors that happen much more or less than expected.

Each category has a brief description and displays aggregated metrics over the traces it contains, including:
- Typical runs stats (like error rates, latency, cost)
- Feedback scores from your evaluators
- Attributes extracted from runs

### Subcategories
Clicking on any category shows a further breakdown into subcategories, which gives you a more granular understanding of what types of interactions are happening within broader categories. In the example, under **Data & Retrieval**, there are subcategories like **Vector Stores** and **Data Ingestion**.

### Individual traces
You can view the traces assigned to each category or subcategory by clicking through to see the runs table. From there, you can click into any trace to see the full conversation details.


## Configuration options
When kicking off an Insights job, you can configure the following:

### Sample size and time range
- Sample size: How many traces to analyze (currently capped at 1,000)
- Time range: Date range for sampling traces
- Filters: Additional trace filters - as you adjust filters, you'll see how many traces match your criteria

### Categories
In the example, the Insights job automatically generated categories bottom-up from underlying traces. In some instances, you might have specific categories you're interested in upfront and want the job to bucket traces into those predefined categories.
For example, with Chat LangChain, you may want traces broken down by:

- LangChain open source libraries
- LangSmith platform features
- LangGraph workflow tools

This will enforce those as the top-level categories that the Insights job generates. Subcategories are still auto-generated by the algorithm, but the top level is enforced. This is useful when there are specific views of your data you want to see.

### Summary prompt
The first step of the algorithm takes every sampled trace and creates a brief summary, which influences how traces get categorized.

There are two ways to edit what the summary prompt extracts from every trace:
- **Summarization instructions**: How to summarize will dictate the types of categories generated. The default is usage pattern focused, extracting the topic or feature the user was using when interacting with the assistant.
- **Trace content**: Determining what parts of the trace are passed to the summarizer. Large traces with lots of metadata can be expensive and noisy. Reducing the summary prompt to only the most relevant information can improve your results.

For example, with Chat LangChain traces, the output contains a `messages` key with the full conversation history, but also includes fields like documents retrieved during the chatbot's response process that don't need to be sent to the summarizer. You can update the prompt to only look at the messages field containing the full chat history, for example: "Summarize \{\{run.outputs.messages\}\}".
