Insights automatically analyses your traces to discover usage patterns, common behaviours and failure modes—without requiring you to manually review thousands of individual traces. Insights appears as a new tab within any tracing project.

## Running your first insights job
1. Navigate to your tracing project
2. Click **+New** to create a new insights job
3. Give your job a name and configure
4. Click **Create**

Insights jobs take ~15 min to complete.

### Adding an API key
For privacy reasons, you must store your OpenAI API key as a workspace secret before running an Insights job. You can do this by clicking the key icon on the top right of the pane that opens when you create a new Insights job.
<Note>Running insights on 1,000 traces typically costs \$0.50-\$1.00 in OpenAI API calls.</Note>

## Understanding your results
Once your job has completed, you can navigate to the insights tab to see your traces organized into a hierarchical structure. In this example, we sampled 500 threads from Chat LangChain over the past week.
![example](/langsmith/images/insights-job-results.png)

The categories column shows how your traces are distributed across the top-level categories that the Insights job discovered.

![nav](/langsmith/images/insights-nav.gif)

### Top-level categories
Your traces are automatically grouped into top-level categories that represent the broadest patterns in your data. In our Chat LangChain example, traces are grouped by the topic of the user's conversation with the assistant.

The distribution bars show how frequently each pattern occurs—you may discover that certain behaviors happen much more or less than expected.

Each category has a brief description and displays aggregated metrics over the traces it contains, including
- typical runs stats (like error rates, latency, cost)
- feedback scores from your evaluators
- attributes extracted from runs

### Subcategories
Clicking on any category shows a further breakdown into subcategories, giving you a more granular understanding of what types of interactions are happening within broader categories. For example, under "Data Retrieval" you might see subcategories like "Web page loading questions", "Document embedding issues", and "Vector store queries".

### Individual traces
You can view the actual traces assigned to each category or subcategory by clicking again to see the runs table. From here you can click into any trace to see the full conversation details.


## Configuration options
During insights creation you can configure the following:

### Sample size and time range
Sample size: How many traces to analyze (currently capped at 1,000)
Time range: Date range for sampling traces
Filters: Additional trace filters - as you adjust filters, you'll see how many traces match your criteria

### Categories
In the example above, categories were automatically generated bottom-up from underlying traces. In some instances, you might have specific categories you're interested in upfront and want the job to bucket traces into those predefined categories.
For example, with Chat LangChain, you may want traces broken down by:

LangChain open source libraries
LangSmith platform features
LangGraph workflow tools

This will enforce those as the top-level categories that the insights job generates. Subcategories are still auto-generated by the algorithm, but the top level is enforced. This is useful when there are specific views of your data you want to see.

### summary prompt
The summary prompt is another important configuration option that can significantly impact your results. The first step of the algorithm takes every sampled trace and creates a brief summary, which influences how traces get categorized.

There are two ways to edit what the summary prompt extracts from every traces:
- Summarisation instructions: How to summarize will dictate the types of categories generated. The default is usage pattern focused, extracting the topic or feature the user was using when interacting with the assistant.
- Trace content: Determining what parts of the trace are passed to the summarizer. Large traces with lots of metadata can be expensive and noisy. Reducing the summary prompt to only the most relevant information can improve your results.

For example, with Chat LangChain traces, the output contains a `messages` key with the full conversation history, but also includes fieds like documents retrieved during the chatbot's response process that don't need to be sent to the summarizer. You can update the prompt to only look at the messages field containing the full chat history, for example: "Summarize \{\{run.outputs.messages\}\}"
