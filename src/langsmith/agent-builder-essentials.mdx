This page gives you a clear picture of what agents can do, how they connect to your apps, how they can start automatically, and how you stay in control.

## Connect apps and services

Let your agent work with the tools you already use—email, calendars, chat, spreadsheets, project trackers, code hosting, search, and more.

- Examples: Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack, Google Sheets, GitHub, Linear, LinkedIn, web search.
- You choose what to connect and can remove connections at any time.

Learn more: [Connect apps and services](/langsmith/agent-builder-tools).

## Start automatically

Agents can start on their own when something happens or on a schedule.

- Examples: a message arrives in Slack, a new email shows up, or it’s time for a weekly summary.
- You can also run an agent on demand from chat or your workspace.

Learn more: [Slack app](/langsmith/agent-builder-slack-app).

## Remember and improve

Agents can remember important details and get better over time.

- Remembers context: Keeps relevant information from past interactions to make better decisions later.
- Improves itself: Can refine its instructions or add/remove capabilities based on your feedback.
- What stays the same: Name, short description, and how it starts do not change without you.

## Helpers for bigger tasks

For complex work, you can split tasks into smaller parts and let specialized helpers handle each piece. Think of these as teammates that focus on one job and report back.

- Divide the work: One helper gathers info, another summarizes, another formats results.
- Match access to the job: Give each helper only the connections it needs.
- Work in parallel: Helpers can progress independently and combine results.

## Approvals when it matters

Stay in control of important actions. You can set your agent to pause for review before it sends a message, creates an event, or makes other changes.

When your agent pauses, you can:
